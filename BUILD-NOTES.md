# Build notes — One-Line Robot Wedding Vows

The entry is a single line of wedding vows for a robot couple, written by a small Python program to the output file the judge reads. The program takes no input, reads nothing from the network, and writes exactly one line of text.

The first version promised love through every firmware update, every dead battery, and every reboot, ending on the warranty expiring. It was valid and on theme, but the payoff arrived late and the line spent most of its words listing hardships before reaching the joke.

The final version keeps the familiar wedding cadence, in sickness and in health, and mirrors it with a machine-specific pair, in low battery and in full charge, so the vow sounds like something actually said at a wedding while staying unmistakably about robots. The laugh lands in the last few words, where the traditional till death do us part becomes till a factory reset do us part. That twist is the whole joke: a factory reset is the robot equivalent of death, and it is also the one thing a robot couple would genuinely fear.

The final line is 112 characters, inside the 160-character gate, and reads well aloud in one breath.
